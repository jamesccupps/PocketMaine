# Roadmap

What is in the game today is in the [README](README.md). This page keeps track of what was planned, what got built, and what is still open.

## Built

Everything on the first version of this roadmap is in the game.

- **New England.** One continuous map from Long Island Sound to the St. Lawrence, 54 ports. New Hampshire and Vermont open at level 4, southern New England at level 6, Canada at level 8. Customs holds a craft for a minute at each crossing.
- **Ships.** The excursion boat sells a loop and is paid by the stop. Freighters and excursion boats need a deep-water harbor. Ferries and freighters fill with groups and lots of ten or twenty.
- **VIPs.** Conditions (no stops, daylight, a sound craft), three VIPs with lists, a plane built only from VIP parts crates, paint that is earned.
- **Events.** A calendar of the season in the Almanac, boat races that move from harbor to harbor, storm relief runs, cruise ships and freighters calling at the new harbors.
- **Holidays.** Eleven, each with decorations, jobs, a VIP, a paint to earn, and a marker beside each port on the map.
- **Saving.** To a file as well as a code.
- **Depth.** Goods with prices that move, port requests, connection jobs, standing runs of up to four stops that hand loads to each other, a rival, fits with a trade-off, crew that improve.
- **An app.** Installed from Chrome it has its own icon, runs full screen and opens with no connection.
- **Lessons.** Aunt Bev comes back once for each thing a level opens and points at it on screen, so the tutorial keeps up with the game.
- **Two paces.** POCKET is real time; BRISK runs the clock three times as fast for a sitting at a desk. Chosen on the start screen, switched in the Almanac.
- **A clock that does not stop.** Time passes while the page is closed, hidden behind another window, or on a phone with its screen off.

## Not done

These need a person, not more code.

- **A test on real phones.** Everything is tested in headless Chrome at phone sizes: layout, taps, the tutorial, a long session. It has been played on one phone so far, and the first thing that showed was that the craft in hand was hard to read; its card was redrawn for that. Other phones and longer sessions are still untested.
- **Balance.** It has had one pass with numbers: a review measured every craft's income, twelve hours away, goods against jobs, and what hurrying every trip does, and the rules were changed where the numbers were bad (hurry, standing runs, overhauls, boat pay, goods, port prices). The first seven levels were then spaced by hand from sixteen simulated games (level 2 at six to fifteen minutes, level 3 at sixteen to thirty-two, level 8 at about an hour and three quarters); a simulated player that neither trades nor answers the rival reaches level 8 in under two hours and fourteen ports in six. At the BRISK pace all of that takes a third as long on the wall clock, so the game is over sooner: nothing was added to fill it. Opening every port costs about two million. None of it has been tuned by a person playing for hours, and that is still the thing most likely to need changing.
- **Old saves.** A save from the Maine-only map is moved onto the bigger one when it loads. That is tested in the rules, but only one save has been through it.

## Ideas, not promises

- **Something to aim at late.** Past level 8 and the last port there are no goals beyond the Hall and the numbers getting bigger. Long contracts, a company history, or a prestige start would each be a real piece of design.
- **More map.** New York and the Hudson, or New Brunswick and Nova Scotia as real ground with their own ports. Each is hand-drawn coastline.
- **More ships.** A second tier of port works to spend late-game money on, and a car ferry that carries vehicles as their own kind of freight.
- **A second species list for southern waters.** Striped bass, bluefish, quahogs. Today the southern grounds use the same five species as Maine.
- **Sound and music options** beyond on and off.

## Not planned

Energy timers, premium currency, ads, purchases, accounts or a server.
